## The three lines, and what each one is actually for

Maybelline's concealer range gets organized differently depending on where you shop, but the same three franchises come up over and over — Fit Me, Instant Age Rewind, and Superstay — which is also how beauty creators walk people through the wall in store [1].

**Instant Age Rewind Eraser** is the famous one. The multi-use version is a sponge-tip liquid in a 6ml/6.8ml tube, positioned for dark circles first: light-to-medium coverage that builds, up to 12 hours of wear, oil-free, fragrance-free, non-comedogenic, with goji berry and Haloxyl in the formula to support the look of the under-eye area [2]. In the UK, Maybelline's Eraser line was the number one concealer by both value and unit sales for the 52 weeks ending 13 July 2024, with Fit Me at number two, per NielsenIQ data published by the brand [3]. In the US, the brand's own claim is "America's #1 concealer," based on a NielsenIQ calculation for the 52 weeks ending 31 July 2021 [2].

**Fit Me Concealer** is the oil-free, buildable everyday one. It's the pick that beauty editors at L'Oréal's own Makeup.com label "best for buildable everyday coverage," aimed at blemishes, redness, and dark circles without caking [4]. The UK shade list runs at least a dozen deep — 10 Light, 15 Fair, 16 Warm Nude, 20 Sand, 25 Medium, 30 Cafe, 35 Deep, 40 Caramel, 45 Toffee, 50, 55 Hazelnut, 60 Cocoa [5] — which is why it's the easiest of the three to colour-match in person.

**Superstay 30h Longwear Concealer** is the heavy-duty one: 10ml, sold in shades like Nude 11 and Tan 45, and named for a 30-hour wear claim [6]. If you're covering something that has to stay put through a humid commute or a long shift, this is the family you're looking at rather than the Eraser.

Then there's **Instant Age Rewind Eraser Color Correcting**, which is a separate product, not a shade. It comes in three corrector tones — green (151) for redness, pink for dullness, orange (159) for deeper dark circles — designed to go underneath your concealer shade rather than replace it, and it's dermatologist and ophthalmologist tested with a vegan formula [7].

## Which one you should actually buy

**If dark circles are the whole reason you're shopping:** the Eraser Multi-Use. It's the lightest formula in its price class and the one most reviewers who've tried both drugstore and high-end concealers keep repurchasing — Amazon's Canadian listing for the Fair (110) shade alone carries 207,170 ratings at 4.3 out of 5 [10], and the Amazon India listing for Medium sits at 4.1 from 126,199 ratings [11].

**If you're covering redness or a breakout:** Fit Me. The oil-free formula won't slide around on a spot the way a glowier under-eye concealer will, and it's the line the category data puts second in UK sales [3][4].

**If your dark circles are genuinely dark:** buy the Neutralizer shade *and* your regular shade, and layer them. That's Maybelline's own step three in the application directions: "for extreme dark circles, apply the Neutralizer shade under concealer shade" [2]. A single concealer will keep showing grey-blue through if you're only building up layers of a light shade.

**If you're over 40 and creasing is your enemy:** this is the honest weak spot. Reviewers in their 40s and 60s report very different results with the Eraser — some say at 48 it doesn't settle into fine lines at all, others say the same formula started emphasising lines around the eyes once they hit their late 20s [10][12]. The formula isn't marketed for mature skin, and the trick that consistently shows up in the positive reviews is under-eye moisturiser first, very little product, then a light powder to set. If you skip that prep, expect the creasing complaints to apply to you.

## Shade matching without wasting $13

The most practical advice comes from the brand's own FAQ: pick your concealer shade based on your foundation shade, and keep two — one a step lighter for under the eyes, one matching your skin for spots, because your tone shifts with sun exposure [13]. A top-voted reviewer on Amazon's Canadian listing makes the case for 115 as the most broadly useful neutral for light-to-medium skin, noting they'd try it in store if possible rather than ordering blind [10]. Take that as one person's recommendation across 200,000-plus reviews, not a rule — but it's a reasonable default to start from.

Worth knowing: the deepest shades in the Eraser range are thinner on the ground than the light and medium ones, which is a limitation the product's own editorial coverage flags [4]. Fit Me's range is broader at the dark end [5].

## Application, in the order that actually works

1. **Twist the collar until the concealer shows on the sponge.** On a new tube this can take a surprising number of turns. Maybelline's directions say so directly, and reviewers confirm it — it's also the single most common "it's broken" complaint on Walmart's listing, alongside the occasional genuinely dry unit [8][2].
2. **For extreme dark circles, Neutralizer first.** Then your concealer shade on top.
3. **Dot it on, then blend outward with a finger or damp sponge.** Reviewers who tried blending with the built-in sponge itself found it deposits too much product; dabbing with the sponge and pressing in with a finger or damp sponge is the technique that gets repeat praise [10].
4. **Moisturise first if you crease, set with a small amount of powder after.** A reviewer who documented a full routine for the 4-star review put it simply: hydrating eye cream, let it sink in, apply lightly, press in, then set immediately — and non-negotiable powder is the difference between "bright" and "cakey" [12].
5. **Don't wet the applicator.** The sponge tip is protected by an antimicrobial system and Maybelline says to wipe off excess with a dry tissue [2].

The recurring hardware gripe is the sponge itself. Multiple long-term users report the applicator peeling or falling apart partway through the tube, and it isn't sold separately — Amazon's Canadian reviews for the Fair shade call the applicator "fantastic" and the same listing notes it degrades over time [10][11]. A handful of Spanish and Italian reviews note slight oxidation after several hours on very oily skin, fixable with powder [14].
